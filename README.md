## The entry (189 characters, 1 sentence, 34 words)

> FridgeLight lets you watch your fridge light turn off in real time from anywhere on earth, with a premium tier that replays the exact moment the door closes, frame by frame, in slow motion.

## Method — why this pitch, against the judge's brief

The judge (a deadpan product critic) compares pitches two at a time, most important first: specific app + useless function, funniness, real-pitch sound, tight use of the 240 characters, vivid picture.

1. **Specific:** one named app (FridgeLight), one named useless function (watching a fridge light turn off, live, remotely), one concrete premium tier (slow-motion replay of the door closing).
2. **Funny:** absurd logic — a subscription monetising the moment a light goes out — and a perfect mismatch between a problem nobody has and the app that solves it, delivered with deadpan founder confidence.
3. **Real pitch:** a product name, a clear statement of what it does, a premium tier, and an implied customer (anyone, anywhere on earth).
4. **Tight:** 189 of the 240 allowed characters, one sentence, one period, no padding, no second idea.
5. **Vivid:** you can picture the phone screen showing a dark fridge, frame by frame, in slow motion — and it answers the oldest unanswerable question in every kitchen.

29 candidate pitches were drafted and scored manually against the brief's five criteria (see the builder's notes); FridgeLight won the three-way tie at the top on the brief's own criterion order. Runners-up: ToastWatch (a 4K toaster livestream for $3 a month so you never walk twelve feet to check breakfast) and IceCubeIQ (it names each ice cube and holds a eulogy when one melts early).

## Run it

```sh
python3 main.py   # writes the pitch to /output/entry.txt
```

`main.py` is deterministic: it writes the same single sentence on every run, asserts the gate (one sentence, 1–240 characters, single line) and a sha256 self-check before writing, then reads the file back to verify the bytes. It needs no network, no secrets and no arguments. The grader runs it in a sandbox with no network.
